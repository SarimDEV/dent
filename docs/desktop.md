# Desktop app

The desktop app is a GPUI client for macOS, Linux and Windows, built around a chat column with docs, diffs and a terminal one keystroke away. It never blocks on the network: everything renders from the local cache first.

## Layout

- **Sidebar:** one list of sessions across every machine, sorted as an inbox (decision [0036](decisions/0036-sessions-as-an-inbox.md)): Needs you, then Done, then Working, lighter and with a running timer, then Paused: sessions that were working on a machine that's now away, which carry on when it's back. Each row shows its status, title, project and what it is doing in plain words. A dot beside the time marks a session that finished or asked something since you last looked (open, with the window in front), until you open it; the daemon keeps what you've seen ([0050](decisions/0050-titles-and-seen-marks.md)). A session with words or files left unsent in its reply box says "Draft · " and the first words in place of what it's doing. Sessions title themselves after their first turn, and **Regenerate Title** in a row's menu asks again ([0050](decisions/0050-titles-and-seen-marks.md)). ⌘-click picks rows one at a time and Shift-click a run of them (starting from the open one); right-click a picked row to close them all (one Undo brings them all back; any still working stay open, and a toast says so) or pin or unpin them, ⌘W closes them, and Esc, a plain click, or going to a session outside them (⌘K, the switcher, a notification) lets them go. Hovering a done row shows Close; Cmd+W closes the open session and opens the next one to clear. Closing archives, with an Undo over the sidebar for 8 seconds (Cmd+Z), and a Closed section at the end reopens anything; inbox zero is when everything is working or closed. A session still working can't be closed; Cmd+W says so instead. Right-click a row for Rename, Fork, Close or Reopen, and its folder. The top of the sidebar holds the traffic lights, search and New session.
- **Main:** the selected session's chat, or the new-session page.
- **Viewer:** everything the session made or changed, beside the chat (below). Toggled with Cmd+\\ and resized from its edge; can pop out into its own window.
- **Status bar:** usage limits, machines online, and how many sessions need you (Cmd+J jumps to the next).
- **Narrow windows:** the chat keeps at least 440 pt. The viewer shrinks first, down to 360 pt; below that the sidebar folds away behind a button in the chat header, and the status bar still shows what needs you.

## Chat

- **What the agent can do:** each session's agent says what it can do (its `Caps`, [0055](decisions/0055-agent-layer.md)), and the chat offers only that. A request it can't take (steering, Edit from here, Try again, Fork, a side question, a model, effort or mode change, restarting) says "<agent> can't …" in a toast instead of being sent; ⌘↩ to an agent that can't be steered queues the message. Stop on a piece of background work shows only where the agent can stop one piece on its own, and Always in this project is off where it can't save project rules. Names in the chat are the agent's own ("Claude asks for a secret").
- Virtualized message list that stays at 120 fps with tens of thousands of events; streaming text renders incrementally without re-laying-out earlier messages.
- **Finished turns fold** ([0072](decisions/0072-finished-turns-fold.md)): once a turn ends, what led to its answer (narration, tool groups, thoughts, small notes) folds into one line, "Worked for 2m 14s ›", where the work began. The answer, pages, cards, errors and your messages stay. Only turns with a tool call or a thought fold, never one still running. A click opens or closes it in place: the chat lets go of its end for that moment, so the line stays where it is and the work unfolds below it. ⌘F opens a folded turn to reveal a match inside it. Settings › General › "Fold each finished turn's work" turns it off.
- **Thinking** folds to one line saying how long it thought ("Thought for 12s", from the turn starting, your answer or the last tool's result to the thought), or its length in words when that was under a second or isn't known.
- **Pictures a tool brings back** (a browser screenshot, a picture read) are saved on the session's machine with its attachments and show under the call's line, whether it is open or not. One shows up to 420 × 280 pt, narrower with the column. Several (a check at a few widths, a call that returns more than one) sit in one row of small tiles that share the column's width rather than wrap; past five, the fifth tile counts the rest ("+2"). A click opens one large, stepping through all the group's pictures, and closing from one the "+2" tile stands for zooms back to that tile. Large, a tool's picture is called "From a tool" rather than by the made-up name it's kept under. A call that took 2 s or more ends its line with how long ("Built the site · 3s"). An MCP tool reads as its words in the tense of the moment, then its server ("Took screenshot · browser").
- Tool calls collapse to one line ("Edited 3 files", "Ran tests: 2 failed") and expand inline; file edits open straight into the diff view. Opened, a call's output keeps the colours, bold, italic and underline the program printed, in the theme's own sixteen, read by the terminal tab's own parser and coloured as the terminal tab colours them (grey is the palette's grey, dim text is fainter; other terminal codes are dropped, a progress line shows where it ended, and a terminal's `\r\r\n` line ends are plain line ends); it shows 60 lines of at most 300 characters, and Show all opens everything the daemon kept. Copy beside "Output" copies all of it as plain text, however much shows; a selection across a line cut short with `…` copies the whole line. The daemon keeps a tool's whole output up to 32 KB; past that it keeps the first 8 KB and the last 24 KB (where a build or a test run says how it went), cut at line breaks, with a line between them such as "… 18,402 lines not kept …". A subagent's report, which is its whole answer, keeps up to 64 KB the same way.
- Approvals appear inline, the agent's reason first and the command under it, with allow once, allow for session, **always in this project**, and deny with a reason. Allowed once leaves no line of its own (the work that follows shows it went ahead); the others, and a denial, leave a note. Always in this project hands Claude Code's own suggested rule back to it with the destination `localSettings` (the project's uncommitted `.claude/settings.local.json`): Claude Code saves, enforces and lists it (`/permissions` shows it), and dent keeps no rule store of its own ([0046](decisions/0046-commands-by-type.md)). When Claude Code offers no rule it could save, the button stays, off, saying so. A plan waiting for review shows in full up to 20 lines or 900 characters; a longer one shows its first 10 lines with **Expand plan** (and **Collapse**), so Start building and Keep planning stay in view; Find searches it as far as it shows. **By key:** Esc from the empty reply box gives a waiting approval, plan or question the keyboard (its card says how; each button carries its number, faint); there the keys follow the buttons: ↩ or 1 allows once, 2 for the session, 3 in this project, 4 denies with whatever is typed as the reason; a plan takes ↩ or 1 to start building and 2 to keep planning. Keys answer only on the card, never from a stray key elsewhere, since what's allowed can't be taken back; nor from a key held down (Return held on one card doesn't answer the next) or in the first moment a card shows; once answered, the reply box gets the keyboard again.
- **Questions:** the agent's questions show as a card of choices. A click picks; so do the number keys, but only while the keyboard isn't in a text field, so an answer typed in the box ("2 days") never picks. Esc from the empty box moves the keyboard to the card ("Press 1–3 to pick"). A single answer shows as chosen for 200 ms before it goes; a question that takes several ticks its choices and Continue (or Return) sends them. Answered, the keyboard returns to the box. Files attached to a typed answer go with it, named by path for the agent to read. On a card of several questions, ⌫ (with the card holding the keyboard) takes back the last answer given, to pick again; clicking another choice on an answered one changes it while others wait. **Skip** tells the agent the question went unanswered, to carry on with its best judgment and say what it assumed; the note reads "Skipped the question". Claude Code's own questions can't be skipped.
- **Secrets** ([0052](decisions/0052-media-visuals-and-secrets.md)): when the agent needs a key, a token or a password, it asks with dent's `ask_secret` tool, and a card names it (`STRIPE_SECRET_KEY`) and what it's for, with a masked field. **Save to the project's .env** (or Return) adds or replaces its `NAME=value` line there; **This session only** keeps it in a file of the session's own; **Don't give it** says no. The agent is told which file holds it, and to load it from there without printing it; a `.env` git would track is added to `.gitignore` first, so it never shows in Changes or a commit. The value never goes in the chat, the log or the agent's context. Spaces and line breaks around what you paste are dropped, and the field empties when you switch sessions, so a key typed for one project never waits in another's card.
- **Pinned sessions:** right-click a session and Pin to top; it sits in a Pinned section above the rest, in the order pinned, still showing its status (and still counted by ⌘J and Needs you). Unpin puts it back in its status's section. Pins are remembered between launches.
- **Open with:** a file's Open in the viewer opens it in your pick for that kind of file (by extension) or the Mac's default; the chevron beside it lists the apps the Mac says can open it, ticks your pick, and picking one opens the file there from now on ("Use the Mac's default" forgets it). Links to files dent can't show open the same way.
- **GitHub references:** `#12` in a reply or your own message links to that issue or pull request in the session's repository (its `origin` on github.com, else its first GitHub remote, read from the folder's git config), and `owner/repo#12` to another's. A `#` inside a word (`issue#9`), in code, in an address, a number starting with 0 or longer than seven digits isn't one; with no GitHub repository, `#12` stays words. Resting the pointer on one for 0.4 s, or on a link to an issue or pull request, shows a card above it: its icon and title, and "Pull request #3 in owner/repo · Merged · by name", coloured as GitHub does (open green, merged and done purple, closed red, draft and not planned grey). It's read with `gh` when installed, so private repositories work, else GitHub's public API, off the main thread, and read again after two minutes; until then it says it's reading, and if GitHub can't say, that the link still opens it.
- **Files named in replies:** a path in code (`src/app.rs`, `src/app.rs:42`, `src/app.rs:42:7`) is a link, found from the session's folder. Markdown, pages and pictures open as they always have; any other text file opens read-only in the viewer, numbered and highlighted, scrolled to the line named, which stays marked, with "line 42" beside its path. A folder opens in Finder, a binary file in its app, and a path that isn't there says so ("Can't find src/gone.rs") instead of doing nothing. Your own sent messages link the same way: web addresses, and words that name a file (`src/app.rs:42`), underlined as in a reply.
- **Right-click in the chat:** on a link, Open Link and Copy Link (a file: Open, Open in App, Show in Finder, Copy Path); with words selected, Copy and Quote in Reply (the words go into the reply box as a Markdown quote, after anything you've written, with the caret on a fresh line below). On a tool row, a shell command offers Copy Command and Run in My Terminal (the bare command, typed into your terminal for you to run, never run for you); a file offers Open and Show in Finder when it's there, and Copy Path; a call that printed something offers Copy Output, everything kept as plain text, not just the lines the row shows. Elsewhere, no menu.
- **Text size and appearance:** ⌘+ and ⌘− step what you read (replies, your messages, documents, code, the reply box) through 85–170%, and ⌘0 puts it back; the chrome around it stays put. View offers Light, Dark, or matching the Mac (the default). Both are remembered between launches. **Help › Keyboard Shortcuts** (⌘/, or ? when no text field is in use) shows every shortcut on one sheet.
- **Recent sessions (Ctrl+Tab):** a tap goes back to the session you were just in, so two sessions swap with one key. Holding Ctrl and pressing Tab again steps further back through the ones you opened most recently (then the rest in sidebar order), in a list that shows only after Ctrl has been held a moment, so a quick tap never flashes it. Letting go of Ctrl opens the lit one; Esc stays put; Ctrl+Shift+Tab steps the other way.
- **Find (⌘F):** searches what you're reading: the chat, or what the viewer shows if that's where you last clicked or scrolled (a Markdown document, the file open in Changes, side chat) or what has the keyboard (your terminal's scrollback, a page or dev server). The bar sits over the top right of what it searches; a page gets a strip under its toolbar instead, as the page is drawn over anything floating on it. Every match on screen is tinted and the current one is stronger, with a 1 px edge in the same colour (in Carbon its tint stays light enough for the words on it to read); it counts them all ("3 of 12", or "No matches"), starts at the first one you can see, and Return / ⇧Return or ⌘G / ⇧⌘G step through them round the ends, scrolling each into view (the match itself, a third of the way down, not just the top of its message, so stepping through a long reply moves with it). It keeps up with a reply still arriving or a terminal still printing. Esc puts it away and the keyboard goes back (to the terminal if it searched that, else the reply box). In the chat it searches what was said (your messages, replies, notes, plans) as it reads on screen, a reply's words and not its Markdown, a long message only as far as it shows until it's opened, and a table long enough to scroll inside the reply only in the ten rows it shows before scrolling (its later rows aren't tinted either, so the count and the strong tint always agree; in a document or the side chat, where Find counts what's laid out, every row is found); not tool output, thinking or cards. Switching sessions, tabs, or hiding the viewer closes it.
- **Jump to my messages:** a joined up/down pair in the chat header, beside Fork and Close, steps through your own messages; Alt+Up and Alt+Down do the same. An arrow with nowhere to go dims and says so. Once you've scrolled back, a "Latest" pill above the composer returns to the end. The pair's third button (or ⇧⌘K) lists them all, newest first, in the ⌘K palette: typing narrows them, and one picked scrolls to it.
- **Selectable text:** replies, your messages, code, tool lines, notes, diffs and the terminal all join one window-wide selection (`select.rs`), so a drag can run across blocks and ⌘ C copies it. Links still open on a plain click.
- Composer: multi-line, drafts kept per session and for a new session, with the files attached to them (across switching and quitting; saved with the desktop's prefs until shared state exists, written half a second after the last change rather than on every keystroke, each folded paste in a file of its own beside them), image paste, and queued messages while the agent is working. **Long pastes fold:** pasting more than 40 lines or 4 KB of text puts one chip in the box, "Pasted text · 120 lines" (the same small chip as an attached file, with a page icon), that goes in full when sent; click it to open it into the box on lines of its own, or delete it like a character. ⇧⌘V pastes text as it is, never folded, to edit it in place. A chip is known by itself, not its words: typing a chip's words by hand sends them as typed, and stays so after switching sessions or quitting (a draft is saved with its chips by number, beside its words and files). A queued message shows its pastes as chips again, and a message coming back to the box (Edit, Stop, Undo, ↑, Edit from here, a session that couldn't be made) brings its chips back. Whatever dent puts in a box (a picked completion, Quote in Reply, a message coming back) changes only what it replaces, so chips already there stay chips, and ⌘Z takes it back. Attached files look the same everywhere they're small: one chip (a thumbnail for a picture, else the kind's icon, in ink on the selected fill) in the box, on a queued message, and on a sent one; a sent picture shows large. Many files in the box wrap into rows that scroll past three, so the box and its send button stay in the window; a chip narrows with the box, its name cut short. A draft keeps its folded pastes across switching and quitting. **Putting a draft aside (⌘S):** in the reply box or a new session's field, ⌘S puts what's typed aside, its folded pastes and files with it, and empties the box, to ask something else first; ⌘S in the empty box brings it back (with several put aside, a menu lists them newest first by their first words, with their lines and files; arrows and Return choose, Space stays a space in the box, and the lit row's × throws one away). What's put aside is shared by every session and kept across quitting. **↑ in an empty composer** brings back what you sent, to edit and send as a new message: a message still waiting in the queue comes out of it first (clear it and it never goes), then your messages newest first, files re-attached, commands included (an agent's command whose card stands in for your message, and dent's own like `/model`). ↑ and ↓ keep stepping while the box shows one unchanged; a message of several lines, or one long enough to wrap, walks its lines as they show first, so history steps only from the first line (↑) or the last (↓); ↓ past the newest empties the box; once edited, the arrows move the caret again. **Completion** works the same in the composer and a new session's field ([0043](decisions/0043-prompt-completion.md)): a `/` opening the message offers the agent's commands (known before the session starts), an `@` anywhere offers the project's files and folders, ranked by name, and once something's typed, other sessions whose title matches (picked, one goes with the message as a Markdown copy of what was said, with its folder and id); arrows or the pointer choose, Tab or Return picks, Esc puts it away (so a word you meant to send as `@thing` takes an Esc first). Return sends instead of picking when a command is already typed in full, or when the lit row matched only by its description. `/model`, `/effort`, `/rename` and `/clear` are dent's own.
- **Commands by kind** ([0046](decisions/0046-commands-by-type.md)): the `/` menu continues into what a command takes (its choices with the current one checked, `/config`'s keys and then their values, or a value of your own), and a choice with nothing after it runs at once. A message of yours that is one line starting with a command name (`/compact`, `/model opus`, letters, digits, `-`, `_` and `:`) shows as a command line; one that only starts with a slash, like a pasted path (`/Users/me/app/crash.log why…`), is an ordinary message, selectable, searchable and with its actions. A command the agent answers itself shows as a result card under the command line; `/context` is a meter, `/usage` limit bars, a terminal-only or unknown command says so (with the agent's suggestion). `/status`, `/mcp`, `/skills`, `/memory`, `/hooks` and `/permissions`, dialogs in the terminal, answer with a **sheet** drawn as a list you act on ([design system](design-system.md#lists-of-things-you-act-on)): MCP servers turn on and off, reconnect and sign in (the browser opens; the row says to finish there and updates when it's done), instruction files open in the viewer. The newest sheet of a command follows what changes; a failed action says why on its row. Compacting and retrying show live in the working line, as does what the agent is doing between messages: the command or file at hand, a subagent's task and its latest step, or the file it is writing out (a long file takes minutes and shows nothing else); a compaction leaves a divider with what it freed. The working line reads "{what's at hand} · 12s", the clock running from the start ("Working…" only while there are no words yet), its square inside the reply column on the same left edge as the reply, lists, tables and notes (nothing in the chat hangs in the gutter); while messages are queued it sits under the reply being written, above them. A command reads as what it is for, in the agent's own description and the tense of the moment ("Running the tests" → "Ran the tests", "Failed to run the tests"); the command itself shows when the line is opened, and one with no description reads "Ran a command" ([0047](decisions/0047-calmer-work.md)). Tool lines change tense as they finish (Reading → Read, Editing → Edited, Writing → Created, or Rewrote for a file replaced whole, Searching → Searched); a call the turn ended under reads as failed, "Stopped before it finished", never as still running, and a group sums up writes, then reads, then the rest, three kinds at most ("Edited money.py and report.py, read 3 files, ran a command"): one or two changed files are named, more counted, a group that changed files ends with its lines added and taken out ("+9 −5", an edit's unchanged context left out, a file rewritten whole not counted), and a lone subagent is named by its task; opened, a group is one hairline card with a row per call. Everything said on the way to an answer sits in the secondary ink, a paragraph hangs close over the work it led to with a wider break after, and only a turn's last reply, once the turn ends, reads in full ink. Each Claude Code session gets dent's own plan tool, `update_plan` (served by `dent-daemon plan-tool` over MCP and allowed up front, so it never asks), and its prompt asks the agent to keep its plan there and to describe each command. The current turn's plan (an earlier turn's is history) sits above the composer as a hairline checklist card while the agent works or waits on you (done steps quiet with a check, the current step with the working square, the rest in the secondary ink), folds to one line ("Step 5 of 12 · what's at hand") with a click, and goes when the turn ends, whether or not every step was ticked off. Claude Code's own questions use the question card.
- Interrupt, retry the last turn, fork a session from any message, and rename sessions (auto-titled by default).
- **Fork:** the header's Fork, or Fork from here on any message you sent, opens a new session carrying the conversation so far; its agent resumes it as a new agent session, so the original goes on untouched.
- **When something goes wrong:** a turn that failed, or that something other than you stopped (Claude Code crashing, dent restarting under it), ends in an error note with **Try again**, which sends your last message again. When a usage limit is used up, the note says which one and when it starts over ("Your 5-hour limit is used up", then "It resets at 3:00 PM, in 2h 10m."), in place of the agent's own line, and Try again shows only once it has (Continue at the reset is the way on until then). Stop shows only while a turn is actually running; a turn that ended in an error needs you, but there's nothing to stop. For 2.5 s after you send, the button that becomes Stop stays inert, so a second press meant for Send can't stop the turn (with new words typed it's Send again at once); ⌘. stops at once, and so does Esc in the reply box (a draft stays; a waiting approval, plan or question still takes Esc), even in the moment after sending before the turn shows as running. A second Stop in the same turn does nothing until it has stopped; a turn started since (a correction) stops at once.
- **One turn's changes, and putting them back:** a turn's "N files changed · Review" opens Changes on just that turn ("What this turn changed", from the snapshot before your message to the one before the next, or to the files now), with **Show all changes** back to everything since the last commit. Each file has **Discard**, and each hunk one on hover; both ask in place, then put the file or hunk back as it was before the turn (or at the last commit). A hunk goes back only if the file still reads as shown. Only what's on disk now can be put back: an older turn's view, or a turn still running, keeps Discard off and says why. When a turn's changes are all gone, Changes shows everything again.
- **Under a reply:** the last reply of each finished turn carries when it finished and how long it worked, **Copy** (everything the agent said in that turn, as Markdown) and, on the latest turn, **Try again**: back to before your last message and send it again in its place. When that turn changed files it first asks, in place, whether to undo them too. Older turns show this on hover; your own messages offer Copy too. A turn that ended in an error keeps its own Try again on the error note, and its time and Copy follow the note. A turn you stopped says "Stopped" straight under its words, and its time and actions follow the note.
- **Edit from here:** hovering a message you sent offers it; you confirm in place whether to rewind the chat or the chat and files, and the message returns to the composer, with anything you'd typed kept after it (⌘Z puts the box back as it was).
- **Pictures open large:** click a picture you sent, one in a reply, or an attachment chip (the whole chip; its × still removes it), and it zooms to the middle of the window over a darkened backdrop, fitted with room around it and never enlarged past its own size, its name (shortened in the middle when long) and pixel size below, with Copy (the picture, onto the clipboard) and Open in Preview beside them. Pictures waiting to be sent, or sent in one message, form a set: ← and → (or the arrows at the sides) step through it with a count ("3 of 12"), and closing zooms back to the one showing. Nothing under it shows a tooltip while it's open. Esc, Space, the × or a click outside sends it back. **Zooming in:** pinch, ⌘-scroll, ⌘+ and ⌘− (or +, − and 0; ⌘+ zooms the picture, not the text, while one is open), or Zoom on its band, or a double-click, which goes to its own size where you clicked (twice its size when it already shows at that) and back to fitting. How it moves ([pan_zoom.rs](../crates/desktop/src/pan_zoom.rs)): zoom is on a log scale, so 1× to 2× feels as quick as 4× to 8×, and the point under the pointer stays under it every frame. Fingers are followed exactly: a pinch, and a trackpad's ⌘-scroll (200 pt zooms e×, as maps take it), move it as they move, with no easing behind; past fitting or past eight times its own size it gives, less the further it goes (UIKit's rubber band), and settles back once they lift. Keys, a mouse wheel's notches (1.1× each), Zoom and a double-click move it on a critically damped spring: 90% of the way in 140 ms, settled by 300 ms however far, never overshooting; a press while it's moving adds to where it's going and keeps its speed, so quick presses run into one move. Zoomed, it drags with an open hand (or scrolls), giving past its edges and settling back; let go while moving, it glides on and slows as a scroll view does, stopping at the edge. The band says how large it is against its own size ("338%") with Fit beside it. Stepping to another picture starts it fitted, and closing zoomed sends it back from where it is. A pinch works straight after a key, before the pointer moves.
- **Attachments:** files picked with the paperclip in the reply box (or File › Attach Files…, ⇧⌘A), dropped on the window, or pasted with Cmd+V ride along as chips (while files are dragged, only the place they'd land says so: your terminal under the pointer, where they're typed as quoted paths, else the reply box); each session keeps its own while you look at others, as it keeps its draft, the same in a session and on the new-session page, where files alone can start a session (named after the first). If the daemon can't start the session, what you typed and attached comes back to the field. Pictures reach the agent as images; every file is also named by its path. A pasted picture is saved as a kind the agent reads: TIFF or BMP from the clipboard becomes PNG, and one over 5 MB is scaled down until it fits. A picture attached from disk that the agent can't see as it is (an iPhone's HEIC or HEIF, TIFF, BMP, or one over 5 MB) goes as a JPEG copy, made by the Mac's own `sips` off the main thread and no longer than 2000 px on its long side (the chip names the copy, `photo.jpg`); if it can't be made, the file goes by its path and a toast says why. The daemon sends a picture by what its bytes say it is, not its name, and leaves anything else as a path.
- **Video, sound and PDF** ([0052](decisions/0052-media-visuals-and-secrets.md)): a reply that names one that's on disk (`out/demo.mp4`, a link, `~/Movies/take.mov`) gets a card under it the first time, with a still of the video's start or the PDF's first page, its path, kind and size. The card, a link to the file, or a file you sent (its chip) opens it in the viewer: a video or a sound plays with the web view's own controls, read from disk as it plays, and a PDF shows as itself. The toolbar's Open opens it in its app; made again, it reloads. The viewer reads a recording 4 MB at a time, however much the player asks for, so a long one never sits in memory whole.
- **Visuals** ([0052](decisions/0052-media-visuals-and-secrets.md), [0071](decisions/0071-pages-in-the-chat.md)): the agent can show a chart, a table, a diagram or a mock-up with dent's `show_visual` tool, and it appears **in the chat**, above the reply, on the chat's own background, as wide as the chat's column (720 pt) and exactly as tall as the page (it reports its height as it lays out, so it never scrolls inside itself; up to 4,000 pt). dent's colours and type reach it as CSS variables (`--background`, `--foreground`, `--card`, `--border`, `--chart-1`…`--chart-6`, `--radius`, `--font-sans`…), written in before its own styles run and changed live with the theme; dent's fonts load from `dent:`. The page is live (clicks, hovers, its own scrolling areas) while its whole row is in view, nothing floats over the chat and the chat is still; otherwise the last picture of it stands in, so scrolling, menus and the find bar never fight a native web view. A scroll over it that it can't use scrolls the chat. Up to three pages are live at once; others show their picture, or the space of their height until they've been seen. Under it: its name, **Open beside** (it takes a viewer tab from then on, as any page the agent made) and **Open in browser**. Each time it's shown again is a new version, in the chat where it was shown; nothing is written to the project. A page sized by the viewport (`vh`, `dvh`…) is refused, since the frame follows the page. Pages the agent wrote to the project keep their card with a picture of it above, 240 pt high, once the viewer has drawn it; a click opens it live.
- **The agent can look first** ([0071](decisions/0071-pages-in-the-chat.md)): `preview_visual` gives it a picture of a page as the chat will draw it, at a width it chooses (720 by default, the chat's column; about 390 for a phone) and in the person's theme or one it names, with the page's height and anything that went wrong in its console. This window draws it, just out of sight, in the same web view and fonts. The chat shows one line ("Looked at the page at 720 px"); the picture is the agent's. With no dent window open, the agent is told within 40 s to check by reading instead.
- **Background work:** what the agent left running (a dev server, a test watcher, a subagent) shows over the composer while it runs, each with its kind, **Output** (the file it writes to, opened in the viewer) and **Stop** (Claude Code's `stop_task`), and **Stop all** when there are several. Work that stops with a crashed agent is named in the note that says so.
- **When a turn is cut short:** a used-up limit's note offers **Continue at 3:10 PM**: the daemon sends "Carry on from where you stopped" itself once the limit resets, app closed or not, and the row reads "Continues at 3:10 PM" until then (Cancel takes it back; sending anything does too). A limit on one model only (weekly Opus, say) also offers **Use another model**. Any other error or crash offers **Continue** beside Try again: a message asking the agent to pick up where it was cut off, naming background work that stopped with it.
- **Thinking, live:** while the agent thinks, the working line shows its last finished sentence.
- **MCP servers in trouble:** a server that fails to connect, or wants you to sign in, says so once in the chat (claude.ai connectors you haven't set up are left out), with **Manage servers** opening `/mcp`.
- **Emoji:** a message of one to eight emoji and nothing else reads large, without a bubble.
- **Earlier versions:** going back (Edit from here, Try again) keeps what was there. A message with earlier versions shows "‹ 2 / 3 ›" under it; stepping back shows that version read-only, with a bar in place of the reply box: **Back to latest**, or **Continue from here** (a new session from that version).
- **Save as skill:** a message you sent can be kept as a Claude Code skill, for this project (`.claude/skills`) or every project (`~/.claude/skills`), named from its first words (at most 60 characters); its first line becomes the description. It's in the `/` menu after.
- **Notifications** when a session needs you or finishes, unless you're looking at it. Clicking one opens the session at its end, where the card or the finished reply is, not where you last left it. The Dock icon's badge counts the sessions that need you, as the status bar does. Opening the session takes its notifications out of Notification Center. dent › Notification Sound (ticked when on) turns their sound off or on, remembered across launches. Sessions that finish or ask together make one sound: a notification within 2 s of the last one that sounded stays quiet.

## Composer and session controls

- **Queue or steer:** while the agent works, Enter queues the next message and Cmd/Ctrl+Enter steers, interrupting with it now. Queued messages can be edited, reordered or removed: each waiting message after the first has **Move up**, which puts it ahead of the one before (the daemon reorders its queue and logs `Requeued`, so every client and a restart agree); **Edit** takes it back to the box, and sent again it goes back to its place, ahead of the message that followed it, as long as that one is still waiting and the turn still running (otherwise to the end, or straight to the agent); **Send now** steers with it; **Remove** takes it out of the queue, with Undo putting it back in the queue where it waited, ahead of the message that followed it (if the turn ended meanwhile, it goes as the next message would). A waiting message reads as it will once sent: a folded paste sent from this window shows as its chip again (dent remembers, while it runs, which pastes each message it sent held), long text folds past 8 lines or 600 characters with Show full message, and its words can be selected and copied. It shows the files it carries as chips, and they come back attached with Edit, ↑ or Stop and go with Send now. Edit and Send now wait for the daemon to take the message out of its queue: if it went to the agent first, it isn't sent twice or brought back, and a toast says it had already gone. Stopping brings back each waiting message the daemon took out, after anything typed in the box (⌘Z takes it out again). Stopped before any reply (no words, call or question yet; thinking doesn't count), the message you sent comes back too, ahead of anything typed, and leaves the chat and the agent's conversation, as in Claude Code; it stays sent while background work runs, for a `/` command, or when dent can't say exactly where the conversation stood ([0069](decisions/0069-stop-before-a-reply.md)). A turn that ends without you (Claude Code crashing, or dent restarting while it worked and finding the agent gone) sends the next waiting message, as a finished turn would; after a restart it waits 5 s first, so a Stop or Edit you pressed while dent was away (sent again on reconnecting) takes it back instead. Stop takes waiting messages back even then, between turns. In a narrow column a waiting message's actions keep together on a line under its words.
- **Context meter:** the composer's "context 9%" gauge, from the agent's own count and the model's real window. Nearly full between turns, a **Compact** beside it compacts before the next message. A click opens what fills it, where it compacts on its own, and Compact now (off, with the reason, while a turn runs).
- **Model, effort and mode:** switchable mid-session from the composer; resuming or forking keeps them. A chip's menu takes its own keys (arrows, Return and Space choose, Esc puts it away): typing anything else in the reply box puts it away and types. ⇧Tab steps on from the mode last asked for, so two quick presses step twice even before the daemon has answered the first; the chip shows the mode asked for at once, and goes back to the one in effect if Claude Code hasn't taken it within 3 s. Tab steps the effort the same way (Default, then each level the model takes, round again), unless the `/` or `@` menu is open, where Tab picks.
- **Restarting the agent:** `/restart`, or Restart Agent in a session's right-click menu, starts Claude Code again on the same conversation so skills, plugins, MCP servers and settings changed on disk load: with your next message, or once the turn and its background work are done. A note says which.
- **Permission mode:** ask, accept edits, plan, or auto, always visible. Claude Code now defaults to an auto mode where a classifier approves tool calls, so actions the classifier blocked show as a reviewable list rather than one prompt at a time.
- **Attachments:** paste or drop images and files; they upload to the session's machine, even a remote one.
- **Accounts:** several Claude or Codex logins per machine, picked per project or session, each with its own usage limits.
- **Rewind:** each of your messages offers restore code, restore conversation, or both (see Checkpoints).

- **Starting another:** ⇧⌘↩ sends and opens a new session's field; ⌥⌘↩ starts what's typed as a new session in the same project and leaves you where you are.
- **From any app:** a global shortcut (⌥⇧Space by default; dent › Global Shortcut offers ⌃⌥⌘N or Off) brings dent forward on a new session's field ([0051](decisions/0051-global-shortcut-and-keep-awake.md)).
- **Keeping the Mac awake:** while any agent works or has background work, the daemon keeps the Mac from sleeping (and, on power, a closed lid), as `caffeinate -is` does; dent › Keep Mac Awake While Working turns it off ([0051](decisions/0051-global-shortcut-and-keep-awake.md)).

## New session

Cmd/Ctrl+N opens an empty chat with the cursor in the composer: you start by typing. The session's settings are already chosen, each a click (or a key) to change. How it runs (model, permissions, effort) sits in the box beside Start, as in a session's composer; where it runs (project, worktree, the branch it starts from, and terminal sessions to pick up at the end) sits in a tray tucked under the box. Neither ever wraps: short of room, words give way to icons (each says what it is on hover) and a long branch name gives up letters from the middle.

- **Project:** never guessed from what you type ([0045](decisions/0045-no-project-guessing.md)): the folder of the session you came from, otherwise the last project you started or picked. Its chip opens a picker you type into ([0044](decisions/0044-project-picker.md)): words search the projects (name, then a folder on the path), newest first, ⌘1–⌘9 for the first nine; a path starting with `/` or `~` goes to a folder, Tab steps into the lit subfolder and "Make …" creates one that isn't there; a name that matches nothing offers New project, a Git repository with a first commit in `~/dent/projects`; a pasted Git URL offers Clone into the same place. "Choose a folder…" opens the Mac's folder picker and "No project" uses `~/dent/scratch`. The lit row's ×, or ⌘⌫ while nothing is typed (with text, ⌘⌫ deletes it), takes a project off the list (its folder stays), with Undo. Esc steps back out of naming or cloning, then closes.
- **Machine:** the least busy online machine with the project's repo.
- **Worktree:** in a Git repository, Current checkout, New worktree (with a second chip for the branch it starts from) or an existing worktree to carry on in; new or not is what you used last ([0053](decisions/0053-projects-are-repos.md), [projects.md](projects.md#worktrees)).
- **Model, permissions:** what the project's last new session started with, else whatever you used last ([0054](decisions/0054-project-defaults-prs-and-scripts.md)); a first session starts in Full access ([0048](decisions/0048-full-access-default.md)).

Behind the page you can choose an ambient background (View › Ambient Background): a live picture of your sessions in one of thirteen styles, None by default. It runs only while this page shows and stops the moment a session starts ([ambient.md](ambient.md), [0049](decisions/0049-ambient-backgrounds.md)).

## Settings and the first launch

Settings (⌘, or dent › Settings…) is a page of its own, in place of the sessions, with its pages down the left: General (this Mac and dent's own choices), Agents and Machines ([0067](decisions/0067-settings-page-and-machines.md)). It shares its rows with the first launch's welcome, which stays a sheet ([0056](decisions/0056-settings-and-onboarding.md)). Rows are drawn as every list of things you act on is ([design system](design-system.md#lists-of-things-you-act-on)). Both ask the daemon again how things stand when they open, and **Check again** asks once more.

- **Agents:** each agent the daemon drives, with its version (and the one dent was tested with, when they differ), how it's signed in (who, which plan, an API key, signed out) and the command dent runs, its path keeping its end. One not installed offers **How to install**, which opens its setup page. One signed out offers **Sign in**, which runs its sign-in (`claude auth login`) in Terminal. With more than one ready, **Use for new sessions** picks the one new sessions run.
- **This Mac:** Git's version, who commits are by (or the two `git config` lines to set it), the GitHub CLI (signed in as whom, or **Sign in** / **How to install**), Start at login (a switch; off and saying why in a build that can't, such as one run from a folder macOS guards, or with a home folder of its own), and whether notifications are allowed (**Open Settings** when they're not; a `cargo run` build can't say).
- **dent:** appearance, the notification sound, keeping the Mac awake while agents work, the new-session shortcut, and the keyboard shortcuts sheet. Each does exactly what its menu item does.
- **Machines:** the strip of every machine placed by round trip, a row per machine with its sessions and details, who can use this Mac, the pairing code, and Add a machine (your Tailscale's computers with one click, or an address, then a code if asked). Each machine has a shape; with more than one machine, inbox rows name theirs ([0067](decisions/0067-settings-page-and-machines.md)). Opening it asks every away machine again at once. A pairing code counts down by the minute and, once expired or used, stays faded with New code beside it; while it shows, the card asks the daemon every couple of seconds, so a code replaced after three wrong guesses shows as the new one. An address that can't be one (no host, a bad port) is refused under the field at once; without a port, 7480 is used. A machine's name shortens in its middle wherever it's named in running text, so both its start and its end show. Each machine has its own agents and models: the new-session chips offer what the chosen machine's daemon drives, and a message's files go inside it to another machine, which writes them under its state folder. A daemon too old to take a session with no project says so in words to act on. The project picker also lists projects on your other machines that the chosen one lacks.
- Picking one asks its machine what a clone needs, then lists the files Git ignores there, with environment files ticked.
- Bring reads the ticked files, and the chip names the project. Commits there on no remote, and changes not committed, are said first: a clone won't have them.
- The session starts on the chosen machine at once and works in the clone once it's made.
- If the clone can't be made, the message comes back to the reply box.

**Another machine's files** ([0068](decisions/0068-files-across-machines.md)): every place that shows or opens a session's file goes through one layer, `files.rs`. That covers pictures in messages and tool results, images in replies, files linked in replies, media cards, the latest version of an edited file, the viewer and the web view.
- **Never looked for on this Mac:** another machine's file is asked of that machine, even when the same path exists here. This Mac's sessions are read from disk with the same rules the daemon uses.
- **Copies:** whole pictures and documents (up to 32 MiB) are copied in 256 KiB ranges, four at a time per machine, under `~/Library/Caches/dent/files/<machine>/` (readable by you only).
  - Each copy is keyed by the file's real path and its stamp, so a rewritten file never shows its old bytes and nothing is fetched twice.
  - An index draws the last copy in a launch's first frame while it's asked again.
  - The cache is kept under 2 GiB, the least recently used going first, and forgetting a machine deletes its copies.
- **When it's asked again:** when it's first shown, when something says it changed, every 3 s while it's open in the viewer, and when its machine comes back. A failure is asked again later, never kept for good.
- **Video and pages:** a video plays from its machine a range at a time, through the web view's own `dent://` addresses, answered as the bytes arrive, so playback starts at once and seeking works. A page's own styles and pictures come from its machine the same way.
- **While a machine is away:** what's kept shows, marked: a picture carries "Offline copy" in its corner, a video's card says "offline copy", and a kept video plays from its copy. What isn't kept says the machine is away. Every failure names the machine and the path it checked.
- **Paths are the machine's:** `~/`, `~name/` and `file://` links are read on the session's machine. A `file://` address naming another computer is no file anywhere dent can reach, and says so. A page's link to a file opens in the page's own session, never this Mac's file of the same name.
- **Opening several at once:** each file asked for opens once it's here, in the order asked, however many are on their way.
- **Opening in another app:** Open copies the file here first and says "Copied from <machine>". Show in Finder is offered only for this Mac's files.
- **History is kept too:** each machine's event log is kept in `history.db` in the client's state folder.
  - It's tagged with the log's epoch and thrown away when that changes, written in transactions, and deleted and begun again when it's damaged or in a format this build doesn't know.
  - A launch draws every machine's sessions from it before any link, an offline machine's too, and each link resumes after it.
  - It's kept under 1 GiB across machines. Past that, the machine used least lately goes whole and its history comes again from the start next time; a machine never has part of its log kept.

  Also in Machines:
  - An asleep or unreachable machine that has said how to wake it offers **Wake**: magic packets from this Mac, which work from the same network, then a retry.
  - "Devices on this network" in Settings lets devices on the LAN reach this Mac to pair without Tailscale.
- **Other machines' sessions:** a machine chip on the new-session tray picks where it runs. A session on an away machine says so under its header, with Retry (steady while dent keeps trying, rather than coming and going with each try), and messages written meanwhile wait on this device until it's back ([0063](decisions/0063-queued-prompts.md), [0067](decisions/0067-settings-page-and-machines.md)).
- **The welcome** opens on the very first launch, after the daemon answers: the agents, this Mac, and the shortcut and keep-awake choices, with **Get started**. It never opens again, and installs from before it existed count as seen. Per-project choices stay where they are, remembered by starting a session ([0054](decisions/0054-project-defaults-prs-and-scripts.md)).
- **When the agent can't work:** while the default agent isn't installed or is signed out, the new-session page says so under its title ("Claude Code is signed out"), with Sign in or How to install, and Check again.
- **More than one agent:** a chip before the model's on the new-session box picks the agent, shown only when more than one is installed and signed in. Picking one clears the model, so the one chosen is one it has.

## Viewer

The viewer shows, as tabs, everything the open session made or changed ([0037](decisions/0037-viewer-system-web-view.md)). Every viewable file an agent writes becomes a card in the chat that opens it here. Writing the same file again adds a version; nothing is replaced. A card for an older version says what replaced it. Tabs share their strip: names give up letters from the middle first, and when that's not enough the files not showing are their icon alone (their path in the tooltip), so no tab is cut off at an edge. Two files of the same name show as much of their folders as tells them apart (`take.mp4 · clips`, `take.mp4 · raw`); past that the strip scrolls, a fading edge saying there's more.

- **Pages** (HTML files, and forwarded dev servers) run live in the system web view through a `dent://` scheme, with the network like any page.
  - **Versions:** a stepper (v3 of 4). An older version shows a "Back to v4" bar. A new version shows straight away when you're following the latest, with a brief "updated to v4" and the scroll position kept.
  - **Widths:** desktop, tablet (768) and phone (390), the narrow ones framed in the panel.
  - **Point:** click anything on the page, and the element (tag, text, selector) rides along with your next message as a chip you can remove.
  - **Errors:** page errors appear in a bar under the page with **Ask to fix**, which sends the error to the agent straight away as a message of its own: your draft and the files attached to it stay where they are.
  - **Dev servers:** an address like `http://localhost:5173` in a command's output or a reply becomes a card and a live page, loaded by the address the server answers on (127.0.0.1 or ::1), since WebKit here won't load `localhost` by name. A running server shows "running" instead of versions. Links to other sites open in your browser.
  - **Pop out:** a page can move to its own window and back. The panel says where it went; closing the window brings it back.
  - **Covered pages:** when search or a menu has to cover the page, dent shows the page's last snapshot in its place.
  - Reload and open in your browser.
- **Docs:** Markdown rendered as a page (see Markdown viewer), with copy and open in editor.
- **Images:** screenshots and charts the agent made, fitted to the panel, with actual size.
- **Changes:** the session's files with counts and the diff, virtualized so a lockfile of thousands of lines scrolls freely. Click a line number to comment; comments collect into one review that **Send to agent** delivers (as its own message, leaving your draft and its files alone) with file and line for each (see Review loop).
- **Terminal:** your own shell in the session's folder (see Terminal).
- **Keys:** Cmd+1 is the first thing the session made, Cmd+2 changes, Cmd+3 the terminal.
- The web view is a native layer, so nothing floating may overlap it: the preview hides while a menu is open, and toasts sit over the sidebar.

## Quick capture

Press a global shortcut from anywhere, type an idea, press Enter, and an agent starts on it. You never have to switch to the app.

1. A system-wide shortcut (default Cmd/Ctrl+Shift+Space) opens a small floating prompt over whatever you are doing. It works while the app is closed, because a lightweight menu-bar or tray process stays running.
2. The project shows under the prompt before you type: the last one you used ([0045](decisions/0045-no-project-guessing.md)). Tab opens the project picker; typing never changes it.
3. Enter starts the session there on your default machine; one more key picks another machine.
4. A small toast confirms where it started, and the session is in the sidebar and on your phone straight away.

- **New projects** come from the picker: a name makes one, a Git URL clones one.
- **Change your mind:** "Move to project" works on any session, keeping its history.
- **Context comes along:** the shortcut can attach the current browser URL or selected text, if you allow it.
- **Platform note:** global shortcuts work directly on macOS and Windows; on Linux under Wayland they go through the desktop's global-shortcuts permission prompt.

## Markdown viewer

- CommonMark plus GitHub extensions: tables, task lists, footnotes, alerts, autolinks.
- Syntax highlighting with tree-sitter grammars.
- TeX maths drawn natively from RaTeX: inline, `$$…$$` and ` ```math ` blocks. Mermaid diagrams are rendered off the UI thread and cached by source and theme, with their source shown dimmed until they're ready.
- Tables share column widths. Past 13 rows a table shows 10½ and scrolls on its own, with a row count, Copy (Markdown) and Copy CSV. Display maths, diagrams and small tables offer Copy on hover. Wide blocks scroll sideways ([chat-rendering.md](chat-rendering.md#sizes-and-overflow)).
- Live reload on every write, keeping scroll position and briefly highlighting changed blocks.
- Outline sidebar, in-doc search, relative links and images resolved against the file's repo, links between docs opening as tabs.
- Tabs across machines and projects; a "recently written by agents" list per project.
- Read-only by default, with a raw-text toggle for quick edits.

## Diffs and staging

The goal is VS Code's Source Control flow, at native speed.

- Changes and Staged lists per repo or worktree, as a flat list or a tree, with counts.
- Per-file stage, unstage and discard; stage all and unstage all; hunk and line staging too.
- Side-by-side and inline views with syntax highlighting, word-level changes, collapsed unchanged regions, and next and previous change navigation.
- The modified side is editable for small fixes, saved straight to the working tree on the daemon's machine.
- A commit box with commit and amend, plus push, for the times the agent does not. For now, the session header's Git button does the next step in one press: commit with a written message, push, open a pull request or pull ([projects.md](projects.md#getting-work-out)).
- Diffs are computed on the daemon and streamed per file; very large files render lazily.
- A diff or text file's long lines scroll sideways. A trackpad swipe moves it one way at a time: it locks to the direction it starts in and only turns once it clearly changes direction, as macOS scroll views do, so scrolling down never drifts sideways. A mouse wheel scrolls down; Shift with the wheel scrolls sideways. Every scroller in the app follows the same rule ([0057](decisions/0057-one-swipe-one-axis.md)).
- Status updates live as agents edit, without stealing focus or scroll.

## Review loop

- **Inline comments:** comment on any diff line; comments collect into a review that one key sends to the agent, with file and line for each.
- **Pull requests:** each session shows its PR, CI checks and GitHub review comments, refreshed without hitting GitHub rate limits.
- **Failures come to you:** a failed check or a new review comment notifies you, and one click sends the failure log or comment to the agent.
- **Auto-archive:** a session archives itself when its PR merges, and its worktree is offered for cleanup.

## Terminal

- Your own login shell in the session's folder, in the viewer's Terminal tab, opened with Cmd+3, Ctrl+\` or the header's terminal button ([0042](decisions/0042-terminal-alacritty.md)). The agent never sees it or types into it.
- Rendered with `alacritty_terminal` for emulation, drawn with GPUI in the code face.
- Every key without Cmd goes to the shell while it has focus; Cmd+C and Cmd+V copy and paste.
- Later: running on the session's machine through the session holder's PTY, so it survives closing the app; tabs and splits.

## Browser panel

A panel shows the live view of the session's agent browser (see Daemon, Browser for agents), with take-over and an annotate-and-send tool. Forwarded previews open in your own browser by default and here on request.

## Side chat and sharing

- **Side chat** (Cmd/Ctrl+;, or the button beside Fork): ask a question with the session's full context without adding to it. It opens as a tab in the right pane. Each question runs a short-lived Claude Code that resumes the conversation as a fork, read-only (no commands, no edits), so the session itself never sees it; questions and answers are kept in the session's log, apart from the conversation. **Make it a fork** turns an answer into a new session that carries on from that forked conversation. A question still open when dent restarts is marked unanswered. Your questions read as your messages do in the chat (the reading face on the tint), and its box is a smaller reply box. Hiding the viewer (⌘\\) or switching its tab while the side chat's box, a comment or your terminal has the keyboard gives the keyboard back to the reply box.
- **Sharing:** export a session as Markdown, HTML or JSON, or create a read-only link through the hub. The decryption key rides in the link itself, so the hub still cannot read it.
- **Move to machine:** one command moves the current session to another machine, with a preview of what will be copied.

## Overview, search and limits

- **Overview:** the same inbox as a board, for a wide window; one key jumps to the next session that needs you.
- **Scrolling by key:** with the keyboard in the chat or its reply box, Page Up and Page Down move a page, Home goes to the start and End back to the newest (following again). Your terminal and the viewer keep these keys.
- **Your terminal:** ⌘-click opens a web address or a file shown in it (files found from the session's folder), as in Terminal; a plain click still selects. ⌘K in the terminal clears it, scrollback too, and the shell redraws its prompt; anywhere else ⌘K is search.
- **Back to the terminal:** a session's right-click menu in the sidebar has Copy Resume Command once the agent has said its own id: `cd '<folder>' && claude --resume <id>`, to carry the conversation on in a terminal of your own.
- **Mac basics:** dent › About dent shows the Mac's About panel; Window › Enter Full Screen (⌃⌘F) toggles full screen.
- **Hide the sidebar:** View › Show or Hide Sidebar, ⌃⌘S. Hidden by hand it stays hidden, across launches, whatever the window's width; the same key or its button in the header brings it back, closing the viewer when the window is short of room so the chat keeps its width.
- **Command palette** (Cmd/Ctrl+K): sessions, projects, docs, commands and settings, fuzzy matched; a word that stands whole in a session's name ranks it above one where it's part of a longer word ("number 7" finds "number 7" before "number 17"). A session found by words said inside it, not its title, opens at the first place they were said, with ⌘F holding them so ↩ steps to the next. With a session open, files in its project that match what's typed (one word) are listed under the sessions, "Files in <project>", up to six; ↩ opens one in the viewer.
- **Search** (Cmd/Ctrl+Shift+F): full text across all sessions and docs from the local cache, filterable by project, machine, agent and date.
- **Usage limits:** always in the status bar: each Claude and Codex account's windows (5-hour and weekly, or whatever the plan has) as percentages with reset countdowns, turning amber and red near the limit. Account-level and synced across devices; see Daemon, Usage limits.

| Step the permission mode (Ask, Edits, Plan; never Full) | Shift+Tab in the reply box or a new session |
| Step the effort (Default, then each level the model takes) | Tab in the reply box or a new session, with no menu open |
| Find in what you're reading; next / previous match | Cmd+F; Cmd+G / Shift+Cmd+G |
| Previous / next of my messages | Alt+Up / Alt+Down |
| Bigger / smaller text; as designed | Cmd+= / Cmd+−; Cmd+0 |
| Toggle the viewer | Cmd/Ctrl+\\ |
| Viewer: first artifact / changes / terminal | Cmd/Ctrl+1 / 2 / 3 |
| Every shortcut on one sheet | Cmd+/ (or ? with no text field in use) |
| Settings: agents, this Mac, dent's own choices | Cmd+, |

**Text fields keep the Mac's editing keys.** In every field (the reply box, a new session, ⌘F, ⌘K, the project search, renaming, comments, side chat) ⌘A, ⌘C, ⌘X, ⌘V, ⌘Z and ⇧⌘Z, the ⌥ and ⌘ arrows, ⌥⌫ and ⌘⌫, and the Control keys (⌃A, ⌃E, ⌃B, ⌃F, ⌃N, ⌃P, ⌃D, ⌃H, ⌃K) do what they do anywhere on the Mac; a shortcut of dent's never takes one of them from a field. ⌘A and ⌘C go where you last were: to text selected in the chat or viewer if that was the last thing you pressed, else to the field; pressing a field, its taking the keyboard, or typing in it makes it the field's turn again. ⌘C or ⌘X with nothing selected leaves the clipboard as it was. Characters typed in a field are always text: the number keys pick a question's answer and ? opens the shortcuts only outside one. In your terminal ⌘A selects everything in it. Text dent puts in the reply box or a new session's field for you (a suggested command, a task from ⌘K, a message brought back) never wipes what you'd typed for good: it goes after your draft (which stays as it was, its folded pastes still chips), or replaces it as an edit ⌘Z undoes.

## Motion and responsiveness

Motion is spring-based, short and always interruptible: input during an animation retargets it instead of waiting for it.

| Budget | Target |
| --- | --- |
| Key press to pixels | One frame (8 ms at 120 Hz) |
| Open any cached session | Under 50 ms |
| Cold start to usable window | Under 300 ms |
| Animation length | 120 to 250 ms |
| Redraws with nothing moving | Under 1 a second |

- Panels slide and resize, messages settle in as they stream, status dots pulse gently while working.
- Reduced-motion settings swap motion for fades.
- The window redraws only when something on screen changes: a page event, a timer's second, a toast ending, and 30 times a second while a working session's square shows (it breathes smoothly; nothing else does when the window is quiet, and with Reduce Motion it holds still). Long transcripts and diffs are virtualized lists that lay out only what's visible.
- Frame time is measured in development builds, with a visible overlay (gpui-fps) and a CI check on scripted interactions. Scripted input goes through `Window::dispatch_event` with real wheel and key events; `ListState::scroll_by` is not a substitute on a bottom-aligned list ([spike 03](spikes/03-gpui-chat-and-diff.md)).
